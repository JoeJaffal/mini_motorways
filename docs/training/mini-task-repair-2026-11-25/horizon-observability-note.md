# Finite-lesson observability: open diagnostic question

25 November 2026. Source inspection: CityCurriculumEnv always encodes time against an observation horizon of600seconds. Small-city lessons have a configured finite60/300-second endpoint, and compressed training treats that endpoint as terminated (no bootstrap). Ordinary cities continue to use a truncation bootstrap. The observed global vector does not explicitly identify the lesson deadline or training task.

The frozen60/300 comparison already demonstrates identical early visible states and different eventual finite returns. That comparison uses separate runs, so it does **not** prove an identical-input conflict within either active run. Different fixture geometry can also reveal context. Nevertheless, a shared critic across finite lessons and continuing cities may have difficulty distinguishing return objectives; this remains a hypothesis, not a demonstrated cause of regression.

[Pardo et al., Time Limits in Reinforcement Learning (ICML2018)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/pardo18a.html) distinguishes a finite task's actual endpoint from a training cutoff in an ongoing task. For finite objectives, remaining time is part of the state; for a continuing objective, artificial cutoffs call for bootstrapping. This supports auditing our lesson contract, rather than changing all terminal flags indiscriminately.

No horizon-observation, bootstrap, actor or critic change follows this note. The active duration comparison remains frozen. If calibration remains poor after longer lessons, consider an explicit task/deadline context for the critic or a separately tested continuing-task contract. Such a change needs observation compatibility, actor-preservation, terminal-target checks and a bounded comparison. It must not expose hidden routes, future spawns or teacher actions. A critic context would be an engineering experiment, not a claim that the paper validates this project's exact architecture.
