## Timing explanation and unresolved policy

Simulator time and computation time are different clocks. If an edit is made at game-time 60s, CPU inference lasting 0.1 or 2 real seconds need not advance the simulation unless explicitly designed to do so. The environment must define what happens after the action.

- **Clock advances after each completed edit:** for example, edit at 60s, simulate 1s, observe at 61s. A ten-edit road then consumes 10 game seconds. Simple fixed pacing; edit granularity changes the agent's effective speed.
- **Paused construction:** edit at 60s, observe again at 60s; an explicit wait/run action advances cars, demand and the calendar. Mirrors human pause-and-build if that is the comparison convention. Repeated no-time edits require an explicit operational stall/episode limit, disclosed separately from game-rule legality masks; no invented reward penalty.
- **Continuous real-time control:** game advances while model computes. Couples gameplay to machine speed and scheduling; not proposed for the first reproducible experiment.

One-second durations above are teaching examples, not chosen settings. Recommendation: match Mohammad's actual pause/speed rules before choosing pacing. If pausing is allowed to the human, paused construction is a candidate. Specify complete edit versus tool/cell selection, wait duration(s), event interruption, weekly-choice behavior, game-over and artificial time caps before implementing. Reward can remain zero during construction and arrive when simulation later completes trips. Discount and credit assignment must then be interpreted per decision, including zero-time decisions; game-time-dependent discount would require explicit algorithm work rather than assuming standard fixed-gamma PPO does it.

## Reward and evaluation proposals

Reward proposal: score_after minus score_before each transition, with no connection bonus, survival bonus, construction penalty or invented loss penalty. Its undiscounted sum equals the episode score change; PPO's discounted objective is a separate unresolved setting. Verify that the chosen simulator score counts what is expected. Delayed/initially zero rewards are a learning risk, not evidence score-only is impossible and not permission to add shaping.

Human evaluation proposal: same frozen simulator, cities/seeds, tool access, visibility rules, pause/speed convention and game-time limits; record prior exposure and repeated attempts. Report per-game scores and aggregate distributions. Keep validation replays for user review; label best/worst/nearest-mean selection, checkpoint and complete cohort. Training-session extrema mix evolving policy versions unless checkpoint identity is retained, so frozen-checkpoint review is preferred. Subjective review complements held-out numeric results. An inspected final city cannot remain an untouched final test if its outcomes guide later tuning; reserve new final evidence after such tuning. Multiple held-out seeds are needed rather than relying on one lucky game.

Model/hyperparameters proposal: determine action/observation/time contracts first, then present a compact spatial network plus global inputs and a fully enumerated PPO config for approval. No layer sizes, gamma, learning rate, rollout settings, seeds or budget adopted now. Mask-aware evaluation is required if SB3-Contrib is retained.

## Clarification: multiple actions during one pause

User asks whether the model can do multiple actions with one pause. This is a sequential feedback loop rather than requiring the model to emit the whole construction plan in one prediction. No fixed number of edits per pause has been chosen. ## Assessment: is a one-second wait a good starting point?

User asks for engineering judgment, not adoption. Recommendation: retain one simulated second as the proposed initial fixed wait for a short pilot because it is simple and gives frequent chances to observe/intervene; optimality and adequate reaction timing are unmeasured. Cost: a hypothetical 30-minute episode requires 1,800 wait decisions to advance the full duration, plus paused construction decisions, if no early interruption; arithmetic illustration only. Quiet periods may therefore consume many policy decisions, and delayed trips span more decisions. SB3 PPO rollout collection is counted in environment steps, with a configured discount factor (primary source checked: https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo.html, 15 November 2026). This motivates choosing rollout/credit settings jointly with pacing; it does not demonstrate any particular duration is best here. Fixed-gamma also counts paused construction decisions.

Proposed later comparison, requiring separate approval: fixed 1s versus fixed 5s waits under the same simulator/tasks, with discount and rollout horizons accounted for, simulated-time exposure and real runtime both reported. Longer waits can reduce decision overhead but delay intervention; measure score/urgent-event effects before changing timing. Do not add multiple selectable wait lengths yet: that expands the action design before the simple baseline is evaluated. One-second wait remains unapproved; no benchmark/training/configuration change or performance claim.

## Alternatives to one neighboring road connection per action

User says neighboring connections seem like a good plan, then requests other options. Record as positive provisional feedback; no final representation selection inferred while comparing alternatives. All road-edit variants below preserve approved paused construction and must obey simulator physical legality. Engineering tradeoffs are hypotheses/design consequences, not measured learning-performance results.

| Alternative | Concrete action meaning | Benefit / reason to consider | Cost / restriction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neighboring connection (current proposal) | Choose two adjacent positions, including legal diagonals, and connect | Direct control of bends, junctions and incremental repairs; clear local meaning | Many decisions to compose a long route; delayed-credit challenge |
| Straight stroke | Choose start, direction and a bounded length for a legal straight run | Fewer completed road-edit actions for long straight sections | Need multiple strokes for bends; length expands choices; must define full-path validity, stock and atomic versus partial execution |
| Model-drawn path | Choose start, then successive neighboring directions and explicit finish, committing the resulting ordered path | Model determines every bend while supporting road-drag semantics | Still generates each segment unless using a separate sequence decoder; calling it one edit does not automatically reduce PPO decisions; length, stop and intermediate legality need specification |
| Planner-generated routes | Engineering generates legal route candidates; model selects a candidate | Can reduce the policy's geometric search burden and permit faster strategic experimentation | Route generation supplies construction decisions and restricts selectable routes; possible traffic-useful routes may be omitted. Changes approved control scope and needs explicit approval rather than being hidden in legality masks |

Explicit goal selection (e.g. connect a chosen house to a chosen destination, then build toward it) is an additional hierarchy, not a mutually exclusive fifth geometry encoding: it can sit over local edges/strokes or use a planner. Goals can also involve congestion repairs/shared networks, so a house-destination-only vocabulary may restrict play. Need goal completion, interruption, switching and policy/training specification; no requirement that hierarchy means two separate neural networks. Sequential/temporally abstract actions have an established RL framework, but that literature does not show hierarchy is best for this simulator. Primary sources checked 15 November: Sutton/Precup/Singh 1999, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370299000521 ; SB3-Contrib MaskablePPO action-space constraints, https://sb3-contrib.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo_mask.html . Custom variable-length sequence/hierarchy behavior is not assumed to be a drop-in MaskablePPO feature.

Recommendation remains neighboring connections for a transparent first road-control baseline, with straight strokes a plausible later extension if action count becomes an observed bottleneck. Reason: clear rules and direct geometric control, not a measured claim of easiest learning, highest scores or equivalence to a human drag interface. Score-only delayed learning remains a risk. Planner-based route selection is a legitimate separate comparison if user approves changing what is engineered versus learned. No new action, architecture or training adopted; bridges/motorways/deletion semantics remain separate pending design topics. No code, tests, episodes or benchmark.

## User rule: generated routes strictly prohibited

User explicitly states "No generated route that is a strict rule here" and asks whether neighboring connections give the model the most freedom. Adopt a strict prohibition on externally generated road routes: no planner/pathfinder-produced construction route, candidate-route menu or automatic completion of a road path between endpoints for the agent. This supersedes earlier suggestions that generated-route selection remains an available comparison in this experiment. Legality checking remains allowed to enforce physical game rules; it must not select/prefer a route or prune physically legal construction because it is judged unproductive. This rule concerns agent construction assistance, not the simulator's ordinary vehicle routing required to run traffic. The model itself may determine sequences of local edits.

Clarification/engineering analysis: neighboring-edge construction provides fine geometric control, including legal diagonals, branches, incremental repairs and road sharing. Assuming every legal neighboring edit is exposed and enough sequential edits are allowed, sequences can express routes made from those edges without an engineered route vocabulary. It is not uniquely more expressive than an unrestricted model-drawn path generator; those could compose the same edges. Its advantage is direct, transparent control rather than a proven largest possible action space. Constraints from inventory, physical legality and any future operational limits still matter. Fine control does not establish sample efficiency or eventual human-level performance; long construction sequences with score-only rewards remain a learning risk. User is leaning toward neighboring connections but asks for judgment; no neural output encoding, goal hierarchy, bridge/motorway semantics or new run approved by this question. No implementation/training.
