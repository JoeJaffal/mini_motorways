### Investigation: noise
SUMMARY: I analysed 6,065 cached f34 games from gp-6 and cma-6, the 16 validation rows on maps 1500-1549, and 8 distinct fixed rules x 100 test maps; f34-gp2 and f34-gp33 are byte-identical. The chaos claim holds. For unrelated rules, the map explains only 9-13% of per-game variance (interaction SD ~310-350, map SD ~105-120), and paired differences have SD 370-530. The source code confirms one shared random stream: traffic parking-slot draws and placement, offer and schedule draws all come from it (TrafficWorld.cs:76, ContinuousJourneys.cs:44, TrafficControl.cs:648, OriginalPlacement.cs:205). Closer policies have smaller paired SD (CMA: 236 at weight distance ~4.5 vs 353-370 at 6-7; GP mutants at 99.5% token similarity: 209), but mainly because more games come out byte-identical (21-42%). Once two games diverge, the gap is nearly as large as between unrelated rules (RMS 258-369), so small mutations give almost no gradient. Trips are essentially survival time (r 0.96-0.98). Early collapses are 5-23% of games and carry 19-46% of the variance; they are mostly specific to the rule-map pair, not to hard maps. No robust statistic beats the mean meaningfully: per-map rank gains only +0.03-0.04 split-half Spearman, and trips-per-tile and q25 are harmful. The decisive numbers: among decent GP rules the true SD is ~54 trips against per-game noise ~248, so an 8-map ranking is ~72% noise. That alone explains the +200-250 hall-of-fame leads that decayed to -8 and -94 on validation. In cma-6 generations 5-9, candidate differences are undetectable (F 0.69), so CMA is drifting on noise. The cma-6 incumbent (1037) shrinks to about 1006 under empirical Bayes, and the 10 validated samples average 42 trips below cma-1 on the same maps. Subtracting the cma-1 reference raises hall-of-fame variance by 38%, while a population-mean per-map baseline lowers it by 18%. The paired-t stop rule at n=50 has only 4-14% power for true gains of 30-50 trips (it needs observed gains of 95-171), so its firing says nothing about convergence. Test maps 7000-7099 resolve only about ±71 (close relatives) to ±127 (unrelated) trips. My recommendations: successive-halving racing with an empirical-Bayes hall of fame, sequential challenger tests on fresh maps, a convergence monitor that tracks the distribution mean or best-posterior rule, noise-adaptive mirrored CMA that selects the mean, and an optional split-random-stream simulator mode after a 200-game A/B check. The analysis scripts are a1.py-a10.py in <old-machine scratch folder>. The literature I lean on (cited from memory, not checked online): Hansen et al. 2009, UH-CMA-ES, https://doi.org/10.1109/TEVC.2008.924423; Jin and Branke 2005, survey of noisy evolutionary optimization, https://doi.org/10.1109/TEVC.2005.846356; Smith and Winkler 2006, optimizer's curse, https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.1050.0451; irace/F-race racing, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orp.2016.09.002.
FINDINGS:
1. [high] Most of the per-game variance on f34 is policy-by-map interaction ('chaos'), not map difficulty. For unrelated rules, the map accounts for only about 10% of the variance.
   evidence: Two-way ANOVA on runs/f34-* (old planner, test 7000-7099, n=100): for the 4 strong rules (cma, gp3final, gp4, separated), map SD = 106, interaction SD = 347, true policy SD = 96, so the map share is 9%. Across all 8 distinct rules: map SD 121, interaction SD 307, map share 13%. Pairwise r = -0.04 to 0.26 (one outlier: gp4 vs gp5gen3 r = 0.44). Paired-difference SD = 368-528, larger than the single-rule SD (182-409). Note that f34-gp2 and f34-gp33 are byte-identical runs (same rule), so there are 8 distinct rules, not 9.
   implication: Pairing on maps barely helps when comparing unrelated rules (GP vs CMA, or a GP challenger vs the cma-1 seed). The only lever left is more games per decision, unless the simulator's random stream is split (see the CRN finding).
2. [high] The mechanism behind the chaos is visible in the simulator source. One random stream (SimulationRandom) is consumed both by agent-dependent traffic events and by spawns and offers.
   evidence: simulation/Assets/Simulation/TrafficWorld.cs:76 creates a single `random=new SimulationRandom(seed,...)`. Draws on that stream come from traffic parking-slot choice (ContinuousJourneys.cs:44 and TrafficControl.cs:648, so every car arrival consumes a draw), building placement (OriginalPlacement.cs:205; the candidate count depends on which cells are free, so the agent's roads change the number of draws), suburb, colour and lot choice (OriginalPlacement.cs:316-379), upgrade offers (MaterialInventory.cs:37), demand phases (CityDemand.cs:69, 215) and the schedule (OriginalSchedule.cs:22, 26, 96). PLAN.md:177 already says the agent's traffic and choices consume the shared generator.
   implication: The biggest variance-reduction lever is common random numbers: an optional simulator mode with separate streams for traffic, placement, offers and schedule. It needs coordination with Mohammad and the fidelity effort, because the original game uses one MSWS stream. It should be a training/selection-only mode, with reporting always on standard f34. Its effect is unmeasured, but a 200-game A/B check (2 rules x 50 maps x 2 modes) would settle it.
3. [high] The paired-difference SD shrinks for closer policies mainly because more games come out byte-identical. Once two games diverge, the difference is almost as large as between unrelated rules.
   evidence: CMA-6 within-generation pairs (1200 pairs, 8 shared maps, 81-weight L2 distance 3.9-7.3): RMS diff 236 at distance 3.9-5.2, 274, 296, 370 and 353 at 6.1-7.3. Mean r falls from 0.54 to 0.40-0.45 (Spearman distance~RMS = 0.28, p = 2e-23). Identical games: 21% at distance <5 and 4% at >6.2. RMS over diverged games only: 258, 287, 348, 369. GP-6 pairs (6192 pairs; both rules >=700 trips), by token similarity: <0.9 gives RMS 363-394 and r 0.04-0.20; 0.9-0.97 gives 316 (12% identical); 0.97-0.995 gives 234 (37% identical, r 0.63); >=0.995 gives 209 (42% identical). Diverged games at similarity >=0.97 have RMS 289 but median |d| only 124, so the difference distribution is heavy-tailed. A linear fit var(diff) = a + b*dist over the CMA range gives a negative intercept, so there is no smooth Lipschitz-like shrinkage to extrapolate.
   implication: The fitness landscape is locally flat-or-random: a small mutation either changes nothing or re-rolls the game. Small-step local search (CMA at small sigma, GP point mutation) gets almost no gradient per game. Also, since identical games carry zero information, the effective sample size of a close-relative comparison is only the number of diverged maps.
4. [high] Close relatives on the validation maps correlate better (r ~0.55-0.65, map share ~45-55%), but their paired SD is still about 230-345.
   evidence: cma-6 validation (10 best-samples x 1500-1549): map SD 213, interaction SD 195, true between-policy SD 22. Pairwise diff SD has median 272 (range 176-380) and mean r 0.55. Each cma-6 sample vs the cma-1 seed: diff SD 231-344, r 0.37-0.69. All 16 validated rules: interaction SD 228, policy SD 37.
   implication: For CMA and for GP mutants of a seed, use SD ~250-300 when sizing samples. For unrelated rules (GP vs CMA, or a challenger from a different lineage), use SD ~450-500.
5. [high] The distribution is broad but not fat-tailed overall. 'Collapses' (games under half the rule's median) are 5-23% of games and carry 19-46% of the variance, and they are mostly policy-by-map specific rather than map-driven.
   evidence: f34 test: cma-1 mean 981, SD 354, skew -0.16, kurtosis 0.76; 12% of games are below 0.5*median and they carry 41% of the variance. Other rules: gp4 23%/45%, separated 18%/45%, gp2 18%/46%, gp3final 15%/32%, hand 5%/19%. Collapse indicators correlate only -0.10 to 0.19 across the 5 strong rules. Maps by number of the 5 rules collapsing: 46/29/19/5/1, against 38/41/17/3.4/0.4 expected if independent (mild clustering only). Collapses are early deaths: cma collapse games end at 362-1059 s, against a median of 1487 s for normal games. Every game is terminated (0 of 6065 cache games truncated at 3600 s). Trips ≈ 1.30*seconds - 816 with r = 0.96-0.98 and residual SD 94, so trips is essentially survival time.
   implication: Avoiding early failure is where both the mean and the variance live. Survival seconds carries the same information as trips (no SNR gain). Diagnosing collapse causes (autopsy of the 12-23% of early deaths) is the most promising path to big gains, but that is outside this noise brief.
6. [medium] No alternative fitness statistic beats plain mean trips by a useful margin at predicting held-out mean trips. Per-map rank among same-generation policies is marginally best. trips_per_tile and q25 are bad.
   evidence: Split-half Spearman(statistic on half A, mean trips on half B), 400 splits per block. f34 test (8 rules x 100): mean 0.891, wins10 0.890, rank_per_map 0.889, trim10 0.882, median 0.870, logmean 0.836, seconds 0.843, q25 0.617, trips_per_tile -0.111. Validation (16 x 50): rank_per_map 0.350, trim10 0.319, mean 0.311, median 0.290, mapc_median 0.219, q25 0.183. GP s4 blocks with rules >=700 (32 x 8, split 4/4): rank_per_map 0.228, mapc_median 0.227, z_per_map 0.218, mean 0.195, trips_per_tile 0.131. CMA generation blocks: every statistic is about -0.04 to 0.02, meaning no predictive signal at all. Script: <old-machine scratch folder>\a5.py
   implication: Keep mean trips as the fitness, since it is also what gets reported. Optionally rank within a generation by per-map rank or population-centred score, a small gain (+0.03-0.04 Spearman) that also removes the map effect. Do not switch to log, median, trips-per-tile or survival time. Robust statistics cannot fix chaos; only more games can.
7. [high] In late generations, cma-6's per-generation ranking is pure noise. Since about gen 5, candidate differences are undetectable on 8 maps.
   evidence: Pooled ANOVA of the 16x8 generation blocks. Gens 0-4: policy SD 112, interaction SD 232, F 2.88, 8-map reliability 0.65. Gens 5-9: policy SD 0 (F = 0.69, below 1). Per-generation F: 1.4, 6.9, 0.6, 4.3, 0.5, 0.4, 2.0, 0.6, 0.8, 0.6. The split-half rank signal on CMA generations is about 0. Sigma has only shrunk from 0.48 to 0.42, so CMA is drifting on noise, not converging.
   implication: cma-7 needs explicit noise handling: more evaluations per candidate or a larger population once F falls toward 1 (UH-CMA-ES style), paired/mirrored sampling, and reporting and selecting the distribution mean rather than the best sample.
8. [high] cma-6's incumbent is a winner's-curse artefact: the 10 validated best-samples are statistically indistinguishable.
   evidence: cma-6 validated means: 992, 956, 987, 1010, 980, 1037, 918, 971, 976, 1032 (grand mean 986). Per-mean noise SD for ranking = 195/sqrt(50) = 27.6. True between-sample SD = 22. Empirical-Bayes shrinkage factor = 0.39, so the incumbent's 1037 shrinks to an expected true score of about 1006. The expected max of 10 equal policies is 1.54 x 27.6 = +42 above the mean, close to the observed +51. Against the cma-1 seed on the same maps, the 10 samples score -109 to +9 (mean -42).
   implication: Do not treat 'incumbent' validation means as true performance. The cma-6 result is consistent with no improvement over cma-1, possibly slightly worse. Any final comparison must be re-run on fresh maps.
9. [high] GP racing selection is dominated by noise among decent rules. The +200-250 training leads in the hall of fame are almost entirely selection inflation and decay as maps are added.
   evidence: GP s4 blocks restricted to rules >=700: true policy SD 54, interaction SD 248, 8-map reliability 0.28. The racing top-8 on 16 maps reaches only 0.34. Expected observed lead of the best of 32 on 8 maps = 2.07 x sqrt(54^2 + 248^2/8) ≈ 213, of which only 0.28 x 213 ≈ 59 is real. Hall-of-fame history (generations.jsonl): 7fa4b2 went +221 (24 maps), +117.6 (40), +81 (56), +58.5 (72), +34.5 (87), then -8.2 on validation. Its 63 maps after selection average about -37. 5c561e went +247 (8 maps), +161.7 (24), +109.6 (53), +68.8 (69), then -93.8 on validation. The cheap part works: rules under 700 trips (75% at gen 0, 18-28% later) are easy to spot (all-rule reliability 0.94 at 8 maps), and a 4-map screen that keeps the top half retains 94% of the 8-map top-8.
   implication: Spend few games on obvious losers (4-map screen) and many on the contenders. Rank the hall of fame by a shrunken (empirical-Bayes) estimate instead of mean - SE. Never promote a rule because of its early lead.
10. [high] Subtracting the cma-1 reference in the hall of fame increases variance. A population-mean per-map baseline reduces it.
   evidence: For 234 policy-generation pairs with rules >=700 on s4 maps, the across-map SD of a rule's per-map score is 282 raw, 331 after subtracting cma-1's per-map trips (variance +38%), and 255 after subtracting the per-map mean of the other decent policies (variance -18%). This is consistent with r(rule, cma-1) < 0.5.
   implication: If hall-of-fame members are compared across different seed sets, use the per-map population mean (or a leave-one-out mean of all policies played on that map) as the control variate, not a single reference rule.
11. [high] The current stop rule is almost powerless. '20 generations without a validated gain' will fire whatever the real progress, so it measures test power, not convergence.
   evidence: At n=50 with required t > t(0.995, 49) = 2.68, a close relative (SD 230-260) needs an observed gain of at least about 95 to pass, and an unrelated rule (SD 450) needs at least about 171. Power at n=50 for close relatives (SD 230/260): true +30 gives 4.6%/3.7%, +50 gives 13.8%/10.4%, +100 gives 65%/52%. For unrelated rules (SD 450): +50 gives 3.5%, +100 gives 14.5%. Maps needed for power 0.8 at one-sided alpha 0.005: at SD 260, 316 maps for +50 and 79 for +100; at SD 450, 946 for +50 and 237 for +100. At two-sided alpha 0.05: at SD 260, 213 for +50 and 54 for +100; at SD 450, 636 and 159. Validation also reuses 1500-1549 every generation, so the incumbent's own validation score is a selected maximum.
   implication: Replace it with (a) a group-sequential paired test of each challenger on fresh maps and (b) a convergence criterion based on a monitored population or distribution-mean trajectory. The 30-generation minimum plus 20 flat generations should not be read as 'converged'.
12. [high] The test set 7000-7099 (n=100) can only resolve large differences.
   evidence: Minimum detectable effect at n=100 (power 0.8, two-sided 0.05): about 71 trips for close relatives (SD 250) and about 127 for unrelated rules (SD 450). The f34 test gaps cma-1 981 vs gp-4 841 (+140) and vs gp-3 832 are about at that limit. gp-4 841 vs gp-3 832 vs gp-2 811 cannot be told apart.
   implication: Pre-register now any test-set extension needed to claim GP-vs-CMA differences of 50-100 trips: about 300-640 paired maps for unrelated rules (for example 7000-7499). This is a protocol decision, not tuning.
RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. 1. Training fitness stays mean trips on shared seeds within a generation. The ranking may use per-map rank or population-centred score (marginally more reliable and removes the map effect). Do not adopt log, median, trimmed, trips-per-tile or survival seconds as the objective: the gains are at most 0.03 Spearman, and trips_per_tile and q25 reorder rules badly.
2. 2. GP racing as successive halving that matches where the signal is. Stage 1: all 32 rules on 4 maps (128 games); drop the bottom half, which catches broken rules at reliability ~0.9 and keeps 94% of the 8-map top-8. Stage 2: 16 survivors get +12 maps (192 games). Stage 3: top 4 get +48 maps, for 64 maps total and reliability ~0.75 for decent rules (192 games). That is about 510 games per generation, about 2.3 h at 225 games/h if gp-7 and cma-7 split the laptop. Elites and hall-of-fame members keep accumulating games across generations, which is free through the cache.
3. 3. Rank hall-of-fame and elite choices by an empirical-Bayes posterior mean, prior mean + rel_n*(obs - prior mean) with rel_n = s_pol^2/(s_pol^2 + s_int^2/n), with s_pol (~55) and s_int (~250) re-estimated online from each generation's ANOVA. Replace cma-1 reference subtraction with a per-map population-mean baseline (variance -18% instead of +38%).
4. 4. Challenger promotion: a group-sequential paired test on FRESH maps, never reused (for example from 20000+), with incumbent and challenger on the same maps. Only send challengers whose posterior-mean lead over the incumbent is at least ~+30. Look at 50/100/200 maps: stop for futility if the mean difference is <= 0 at 50 or 100 maps, accept if the one-sided O'Brien-Fleming-adjusted bound clears at overall alpha 0.01. Retire the fixed 1500-1549 panel for decisions; keep it only for continuity plots. Expected cost is ~150-300 games per tested challenger.
5. 5. Replace the stop rule's incumbent-improvement criterion with a convergence monitor. Each generation, play the CMA distribution mean (and the GP top-posterior rule) on 16 fresh maps. Declare convergence when the regression slope of this monitored score over the last 10-15 generations has a 90% CI inside ±(a few trips per generation) AND, for CMA, sigma has shrunk or F (between-candidate vs interaction) has stayed below ~1.2 at the current evaluation budget. Keep a wall-clock backstop.
6. 6. cma-7 noise handling: (a) report and select the distribution MEAN, never the best sample (cma-6's best-samples are indistinguishable: true SD 22 against noise 28 per 50-map mean); (b) mirrored/antithetic sampling with each ± pair on identical seeds (close relatives correlate r ~0.5, so this halves the map variance); (c) UH-CMA-ES-style adaptation: re-evaluate a few candidates on new maps each generation, and when rank changes show noise dominance, increase maps per candidate or the population size (popsize 32 x 4-6 maps has the same budget as 16 x 8 and is usually better under additive noise, per Arnold & Beyer); (d) budget about 128-192 games per generation plus 16 for the mean-monitor.
7. 7. Optional, high value, needs coordination: build a training-only 'split random streams' simulator mode (traffic parking-slot draws ContinuousJourneys.cs:44 and TrafficControl.cs:648 on their own stream, placement/offers/schedule on others). Before adopting, run a 200-game A/B (2 rules x 50 maps x 2 modes) to measure whether the cross-policy r rises from ~0.2 to something useful. Always report on standard f34.
8. 8. Final selection and reporting: compare at most 3 finalists per method once, on 200 unused maps. Then test once on a test set pre-registered large enough for the claim. At SD 450, 7000-7099 resolves only about ±127 trips. Declare an extension (for example 7000-7499) before looking if GP-vs-CMA gaps under 100 trips are to be claimed. Use the paired 95% CI and Wilcoxon as in PLAN.md:201.
9. 9. Budget sanity: at ~450 games/h total, ~225/h per method, 48 h is about 10,800 games per method. Under the proposals that is ~20 GP generations or ~60 CMA generations plus monitoring. Prefer fewer, better-measured generations: the measured data says 8-map decisions among decent rules are 70% noise (GP) or 100% noise (late CMA).
OPEN QUESTIONS:
- How much would split random streams (common random numbers) actually raise the cross-policy correlation? This is unmeasured; it needs a 200-game A/B and coordination with the simulator owners.
- The f34 test-set numbers (map share 9-13%, r ~0.0-0.26) come from the OLD planner before the f34 cost fix. Validation data with the fixed planner shows a higher map share (45-55%) among close relatives. Do unrelated rules under the fixed planner also correlate better? A new test run of the fixed planner would show this.
- Is the true between-policy SD among decent GP rules (~55 trips) a property of the current representation, or a sign that the search is stuck near the cma-1 basin? If real headroom is only ~50-100 trips, no evaluation protocol will produce 'far above baseline' results without structural changes (collapse avoidance).
- Do collapses (5-23% early deaths, 30-46% of variance) have common, fixable causes such as a specific shop-overflow pattern or upgrade starvation? An autopsy of these games is needed, and it could cut the variance and raise the mean at the same time.
- Per-map rank was marginally the most reliable statistic, but this rests on modest blocks (16x50, 32x8) and a 0.03-0.04 Spearman gain. It should be confirmed before relying on it.
- The CMA within-generation F < 1 in 6 of 10 generations could partly reflect the training maps (same 8 for all candidates) being unrepresentative. A larger per-generation map set would show whether real differences of ~30-50 trips exist below the noise.

### Investigation: simulator
SUMMARY: Policies on the same seed diverge because one random stream is used for everything after reset. The reset draws (start offset, the whole destination plan with times, colours and multipliers, the first lot) are the same for every policy, so the plan's timing is already common. After that, house and destination placement draws one NextDouble for every legal (tile, layout) candidate, and tiles holding a road are not legal (OriginalPlacement.cs:118, 205). The agent's roads therefore change how many draws each spawn uses, which shifts every later draw: grouping, double-lot rolls, upgrade targets, weekly offers and parking-bay picks (ContinuousJourneys.cs:44, TrafficControl.cs:648). A 'common random numbers' (CRN) mode is feasible as an opt-in flag. Each draw would be keyed by purpose and event (placement weight = hash(seed, colour, k-th spawn, x, y, layout); offers keyed by week; parking on its own stream). That is about 10 call sites in 6 C# files plus a reset field, roughly 100-200 lines, and it can run from a private DLL through EVOLVED_RULES_HOST_DLL. Each draw stays an independent uniform, so a policy's score distribution is unchanged; only the coupling between policies changes. Exact sequence parity, which the fidelity work targets, stays untouched in default mode. The data says the variance at stake is large. Unrelated rules on the 100 f34 test seeds correlate per map at only r = 0.09 (paired-difference SD 435). Near-identical CMA samples already reach r = 0.55 on validation and 0.29-0.65 within training generations. Raising r to 0.7-0.85 would cut the maps needed for a given precision by 3-6x, but the r reachable under CRN is unmeasured and needs a pilot of about 1 hour. Throughput: right now the C# hosts, not Python, are the bottleneck. A live sample showed the 12 dotnet hosts using 10.5 cores against 2.5 for Python (81%/19%). The likely hot spots are O(n²) loops in the per-tick vehicle update. One of them is pure dead work in OriginalReference mode (TrafficControl.cs:344-348): it computes values that this branch never uses. The 900 f34 replays plus `--verify-replay` give a bit-exact regression suite for speedups that must not change behaviour. Cutting games short at a fixed time is useless for screening. Score is almost entirely a function of how long the game survives (trips ~ cubic(seconds), R² = 0.995, residual SD 26). Up to about 800 s, mean trips are the same for every rule (22, 47, 80, 124, 173 at T = 200-600). At T = 1000, trips@T correlates with final trips at only r = 0.67 and ranks the rules at Spearman 0.65. No game reached the 3600 s cap; the longest ended at 1970 s.
FINDINGS:
1. [high] Divergence mechanism: the simulator uses one ReferenceRandom (MSWS) stream per game (ReferenceRandom.cs:31-38; TrafficWorld.cs:76). The reset draws are policy-independent. The in-game draws that depend on the agent are placement candidate weights (one NextDouble per legal tile+layout, and road tiles are excluded), house grouping, double-lot roll, 'any'-colour pick, destination-upgrade target, weekly offer picks plus their shuffle, lazily drawn demand phases, and parking-bay picks.
   evidence: Reset: InitializePlayableArea draws the start offset (CityExpansion.cs:13-16); InitializeOriginalPlan draws all plan times and permutations up front (OriginalSchedule.cs:14-33). In-game: GeneratePlacements makes one draw per candidate (OriginalPlacement.cs:205), and the candidate test needs Tile(c)==Infrastructure.None (OriginalPlacement.cs:118). AssessHouseGrouping (OriginalPlacement.cs:316-317). TrySpawnPlannedDestination colour pick and double-lot roll (OriginalPlacement.cs:348-350). RandomActiveColor (379). ScheduleDestinationUpgrade (OriginalSchedule.cs:96). UpdateOriginalInventory picks 2 offers and shuffles (MaterialInventory.cs:37-45); its weights depend on Inventory, packagesTaken and weeksNotOffered. demandGroupPhases is drawn lazily (CityDemand.cs:215). Parking bay: random.Next(free.Count) (ContinuousJourneys.cs:44; TrafficControl.cs:648). PLAN.md:177 already lists the consequences qualitatively.
   implication: Once the first spawn or parking event happens after the agent's first road (within the first minute or so), every later city event comes from a shifted stream. Two policies on 'the same map' then play different cities. This is the root cause of the r~0.1 between-rule correlation and of the winner's-curse pattern seen in gp-3.3 to gp-6.
2. [high] No CRN-like mechanism exists in the interactive host protocol. 'variation' is only SameMap/DifferentMaps (city choice by seed). The observation's ScheduledSpawns is a read-only view of the destination plan. A recorded-spawns mode (CustomScenario) exists, but only through the --run-scenario CLI, with growth off and recorded offers.
   evidence: TrafficTypes.cs:44 `enum MapVariation {SameMap,DifferentMaps}`. TrafficWorld.cs:72 picks the city from new Random(seed) only for DifferentMaps. Program.cs:134-141: reset takes version, scenario, seed and variation, and nothing else. TrafficWorld.cs:405 ScheduledSpawns = originalPlan Type-0 entries. CustomScenario.cs:37-60 replaces random growth and offers with recorded events; Program.cs:39-60 exposes it only as --run-scenario.
   implication: CRN needs a new simulator option. Replaying recorded spawns is a poor substitute: recorded spawn cells ignore the agent's roads, so events get rejected.
3. [medium] A CRN mode is feasible and small. Key every in-game draw by purpose and event identity instead of stream position: placement weight = U(hash(seed,'place',colour,kind,k-th spawn of that colour/kind,x,y,layout)) × average; grouping keyed (colour,k); double-lot, any-colour and upgrade-target draws keyed by the plan entry's Order (fixed at reset); offers and shuffle keyed by week; demand phase keyed by colour; parking on a separate stream. The marginal distribution of every game stays the same, because each draw is still an independent U(0,1). Only the joint distribution across policies changes.
   evidence: About 10 call sites across OriginalPlacement.cs, OriginalSchedule.cs, MaterialInventory.cs, CityDemand.cs, ContinuousJourneys.cs and TrafficControl.cs. Also needed: a Rules flag, a 'randomStreams' field on the reset Request (Program.cs:25 area, Reset signature), the field in AiReplayRecording so replays verify, and a SplitMix64-style hash. With position-keyed noise, the same placement wins in both worlds unless an agent road occupies the winning footprint or nearby buildings change the weights. CanFindPathAwayFromDriveway only looks at buildings and static terrain (OriginalPlacement.cs:223-262), not roads. The default mode stays bit-identical, so the RNG oracle tests (SimulationChecks.cs:473-496) and the fidelity team's stream-matching work are unaffected. host.py:52-57 already supports a private DLL through EVOLVED_RULES_HOST_DLL.
   implication: Estimate 1-2 days, including a distribution check (cma-1 and the hand rule on 200 seeds in both modes; the means should agree within about ±50). Default off. The cache version key must include the mode (e.g. 'gp7-v1@f34+crn'). Coordinate with Mohammad and the fidelity work, or run it as a local flag-only build.
4. [medium] Measured per-map correlations, which set how much CRN can win. Unrelated rules on f34 test 7000-7099 correlate at mean r = 0.093 (median 0.079; one duplicate pair excluded), paired-difference SD 435 against a within-rule SD of 319. Near relatives already correlate much more: cma-6 validated best-samples r = 0.55 (0.14-0.81), paired-difference SD 273 (176-380), and r falls as weight distance grows (e.g. distance 4.1 gives r 0.81; 6.7 gives 0.14). Within cma-6 training generations (16 candidates × 8 seeds) the pairwise r is 0.29-0.65 and the seed share of variance is 0.25-0.71. gp-6 validated rules: r = 0.31 (0.06-0.92).
   evidence: Computed in <old-machine scratch folder>\trunc.py and <old-machine scratch folder>\corr.py from runs/f34-*/replays, cma-6/cache.jsonl and gp-6/cache.jsonl. Var(paired diff) = 2σ²(1−r). ### Investigation: gp
SUMMARY: GP in gp-6 could not improve on the cma-1 seed, and the main reason is a structural bug, not bad luck. The cma-1 priority tree (159 nodes, 40 weighted terms) has height 7, but gp.py sets MAX_DEPTH = 6 and quietly reverts any child taller than that. I ran gp.mutate 20,000 times offline on cma-1: 99.8% of priority-tree mutations were reverted, and crossover never changed the priority tree. So the decision formula that carries cma-1's strength was frozen for the whole run. GP could only edit cma-1's hold and upgrade trees, plus the constant remove and place trees, which get 40% of all mutations. The only cma-1 relatives that reached validation were of the form "cma-1 + new upgrade + place: 0.4". Constants cannot be fine-tuned either: an ephemeral mutation throws the old value away and draws a new one, a jump averaging 1.11 against cma-1's mean |weight| of 0.59. Selection is mostly luck. Inside the racing top, the correlation between scores on the first 8 maps and the next 8 averages only 0.26. The racing winners led cma-1 by about +95 trips on their 16 maps but are about -10 to -35 on validation. One hall member fell from +221 (24 maps) to +34.5 (87 maps), so its 63 later maps averaged about -36. Unrelated rules barely correlate map by map (r = -0.06 to 0.32, paired-difference SD 334-421). Close relatives that share cma-1's priority tree correlate at r of about 0.7 (paired SD about 230), so local search around one incumbent needs roughly 2-3x fewer games than comparing unrelated rules. The 38 random immigrants never reached the racing top (mean 273 trips, best 744, against roughly 1000 for the elites). A quarter of new texts score under 500 trips. The compact gp-3/gp-4 family fills 18-30 of the 32 slots and sits within about ±40 of cma-1 on 90-104 training maps. The top of the landscape is therefore flat compared with the noise, and formula tweaks alone are unlikely to give the "far above baseline" gains wanted. For gp-7 I recommend, in order: (1) fix the representation, using n-ary weighted-sum nodes with Gaussian weight steps, so GP can edit the strong seed; (2) make gp-7 a GP-over-features plus CMA-over-weights hybrid (the dynamic model), compared against cma-7 at equal game budgets; (3) replace the 8+8 racing and tournament with sequential paired races against a champion on fresh maps; (4) drop immigrants and lexicase; (5) validate rarely, on large fresh map blocks. RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. 1. Fix the representation first (cost 0 games; code and unit tests only). Replace MAX_DEPTH = 6 with a node limit per tree (e.g. ≤200) and a soft height cap (≥10). Add an n-ary weighted-sum root, lin(w1·t1 + ... + wk·tk), where each t_i is a small GP subtree (a bare input by default), so the cma-1/cma-7 linear rule is exactly a lin node with 40/16/23 terms. Mutations: (a) Gaussian step on one or all weights (σ adapted, start around 0.2·max(|w|, 0.1)); (b) add a term with weight 0 (neutral by design, so the parent's behaviour is kept until the weight is tuned); (c) delete the term with the smallest |w|·SD(input); (d) replace one term's subtree with a depth-1..3 random tree (if_gt, mul, min, max of inputs give the nonlinearity CMA cannot express). Expected effect: GP can finally edit the strong seed, and the child-is-a-clone problem (99.8% of priority mutations) disappears. Make sure the cma-7 seed loads as lin trees.
2. 2. Make gp-7 a hybrid ('dynamic feature GP'): CMA-ES over the weights of [the base inputs + K GP-evolved feature terms], with an outer GP loop that adds and removes feature terms. Each 'epoch' (e.g. 5 CMA generations of 16 x 8 = 128 games, 640 games total) ends by pruning terms whose |w|·SD(feature) stays below a threshold. GP then proposes up to 4 new terms (mutations or crossovers of surviving terms, or of the remove and place trees in template form), inserted at weight 0 with their own σ, so CMA restarts at the incumbent. Literature: MRGP (Arnaldo et al. 2014, https://doi.org/10.1145/2576768.2598291), FFX (McConaghy 2011, https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4614-1770-5_13), linear scaling (Keijzer 2003, https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-36599-0_7), constant optimisation inside GP (Kommenda et al. 2020, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10710-019-09371-3). Cost: like cma-6 (128 games per generation, about 78 generations per 10k games). Experimental design: cma-7 = CMA-ES over the fixed 81 inputs; gp-7 = the same CMA plus GP-evolved features, same seeds, same budget. That is a clean ablation of whether GP adds structure value, and it reuses all of tune_cmaes.py. This is the most likely way for the GP side to beat CMA rather than tie it.
3. 3. If a separate tree-GP arm is kept, replace racing 8+8 and tournament with champion-vs-challenger sequential paired races on fresh maps, in the style of Hoeffding/Bernstein races (Heidrich-Meisner & Igel 2009, https://doi.org/10.1145/1553374.1553426) or iterated racing / irace (López-Ibáñez et al. 2016, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orp.2016.09.002). The champion plays each block of 4 new maps once, shared by all challengers. A challenger is rejected early if its paired upper bound after 4, 8, 16 or 32 maps is below 0 (catastrophic mutants, about 25% of children, die after 4 games), and accepted only when it has ≥64 maps with a paired lower bound > 0 at about 90%. Expected cost per challenger ≈ 20-40 games on average (≈131 for a real +50 gain within the family, σd 230). That is about 250-400 well-judged challengers per 10k games, against gp-6's ~400 badly judged ones in ~5k games. Keep the hall's idea of evidence that accumulates and never resets.
4. 4. Keep variation local and neutral (0 games). Draw at least 80% of children as mutants of the champion or hall members, where paired SD is about 230 instead of 340-420, a 2-3x saving in games. Crossover only within a family; island or niche separation between the 'linear seed' family and the 'compact' family, and at most 1 cross-family crossover per generation. Set a tree's mutation probability by its usefulness, not 1/5 each: the constant remove and place trees now take 40% of mutations, with mean effects of -86 to -249 when they change alone or with hold.
5. 5. Drop random immigrants (38 tried, 0 reached the racing top, mean 273 trips): saves about 12.5% of round-1 games, about 32 games per generation. If diversity collapses (behaviours < 50% of the population for 3 generations), inject mutants of archived hall members instead.
6. 6. Give the place tree a parametric template, e.g. if_gt(pl_is_light, 0.5, w1·pl_stopped_near + w2·pl_mean_wait_near + b, -1) with CMA-tuned w, and similarly a template for remove, so CMA-7 can also place lights. Then test 'cma-1/cma-6 + lights' head to head on ≥200 fresh maps (about 400 games). Cost 400 games; possible +20 trips (old-sim lights gain was +20.6 [-1.1, +43.2]). It is the only structural gain GP found near cma-1 (place: 0.4 variants at -11 to +15 so far).
7. 7. Validation and final selection: validate rarely and on more maps. Rotate fresh 200-map blocks (e.g. 4200-4399, 4400-4599), never reused, and check only challengers that passed the race (step 3). This avoids the reuse bias found in gp-3.3 (EXPERIMENTS.md:222-232). Per check 200 maps ≈ 200 games; SE ≈ 23 (relatives, paired SD 230) to 30 (unrelated, about 420/√200), so +60 is detectable for unrelated rules. Keep the stop rule's paired test, but against the champion on the same fresh block, not against a lucky anchor.
8. 8. Budget planning: at about 450 games/h on 12 workers, gp-7 and cma-7 sharing the machine get about 225 games/h each, about 10.8k games per 48 h. Allocate the same game budget to each (the fair-comparison rule), log games, not generations, as the x-axis, and report population (or CMA mean-vector) average next to best. A method-level claim needs ≥3 runs per method: about 30k games per method, roughly 6 days. Plan for that, or say plainly that it is a single-run comparison.
9. 9. Do not adopt lexicase or ε-lexicase, larger populations, or deeper random trees: with per-map r≈0 between rules they spend games on noise. Put effort into representation (1-2) and evaluation allocation (3, 7).
10. 10. Outside GP but decisive: ask the simulator team whether spawn and demand randomness can be drawn from a stream separate from the agent's actions. That would raise the per-map correlation r between rules; the paired variance falls by a factor (1 - r), so r 0.2 → 0.7 means about 2.7x fewer games for every comparison and every final test claim.

### Investigation: cma
SUMMARY: I read tune_cmaes.py, gp.py, features.py, agent.py and EXPERIMENTS.md, and analysed the run data: the cma-6 and cma-1 caches, generations, validation and checkpoints (copies unpickled in the scratchpad), plus the gp-6 cache and validation. I did not run any games. Scripts are in <old-machine scratch folder> (a1.py to a10.py).

Main result: cma-6's CMA updates carry almost no information. The 16 candidates of a generation are ranked on 4 maps; that ranking does not predict their ranking on the other 4 maps (split-half Spearman -0.04, so 8-map rank reliability is about 0). A ridge regression of candidate score on weights from all 1280 training games has negative cross-validated R². The distribution mean has drifted 6.44 (L2) from cma-1 in 10 generations, against 5.8 expected from a pure random walk, and the covariance matrix has learned nothing (condition number 1.17; c1+cmu = 0.00125 per generation). The only real signal is avoiding the occasional broken candidate (for example one scoring 131).

The 'best sample' is a winner's-curse pick twice over. Training best minus validation is +99.5 on average, against about +125 expected for the best of 16 with no true differences. The incumbent of 1037 is the maximum of 10 validations whose cloud has mean 986 and SD 35, which predicts about 1040. Paired against cma-1 it is +9 ± 36.

On average the samples are about 42 trips worse than cma-1 on validation. That is the cost of sampling at sigma 0.45, so making the distribution mean the incumbent would remove most of that handicap at no cost. Two more caveats: 34 of the 81 weights have no effect or are redundant, and the 1027.7 'cma-1 on validation' figure was played with clean routes on, while every cma-6 policy plays with clean routes off.

Near cma-1 the linear policy family looks flat within noise. Ten relatives of cma-1 differ by only about 23 trips SD on 50 validation maps. Very different policies land at the same level: the 29-node gp-6 rule is effectively a 4-weight linear rule plus lights, and scores 1019.5 against 1027.7, with per-map r = 0.14.

So better noise handling makes cma-7 an honest, working control, but not a big win. My best guess is that a fixed linear CMA reaches cma-1's level to about +5% (validation about 1020-1080). Any larger gain has to come from new decisions or features, such as lights, time-gated terms or clean routes, and these should be screened cheaply first.

### Investigation: headroom
SUMMARY: Survival time decides the score, and every rule currently dies at about the same point. Across about 6,100 gp-6 and cma-6 games (gp6-v1 planner, f34), trips follow one curve, trips ≈ 1.283e-4 × T^2.18 (T = seconds survived), with R² = 0.985 (residual SD 38 trips against a trips SD of 308). Rules do not differ in throughput while alive: on 100 test maps, cma-1, gp-4, separated and even the hand rule all have 47, 175, ~377 and ~686 trips at 300, 600, 900 and 1200 s. Rules differ only in when they die. With the fixed planner the best rules have nearly removed early deaths (survival at 1200 s is 0.88–0.92), so removing every death before 1200 s would add only about +4.5 trips (999 → 1003). All rules hit a wall at 1400–1600 s, where the risk of dying within 200 s jumps to 0.8–0.9, and almost no game passes 1800–2000 s. A simple supply model from the simulator's own constants explains the wall: the demand ramp, 1 + 0.013 × (day − 35) from 700 s, plus the shop wave of days 60–110, cut cars per unit of demand by about 30% between 1000 and 1600 s, after which the ratio levels off at about 4.2–4.4. So the round trip each colour's cars can afford shrinks fast, and the main lever is shorter car round trips, not choosing better among the routes already on offer. The curve prices this: each extra 100 s of survival at the wall is worth about +146 trips, +200 s about +315 and +300 s about +496. Road stock is not the limit: games end with 66–95 tiles unused, and only 1.5–2.3% end with 5 or fewer. Picking among the current routes looks used up. The cma-1 hold almost never blocks a build (from its weights), every top rule is essentially 'separated route first, else the cheapest or fastest one', and a 29-node GP rule (−2·path_cost_s + 15.6·is_separated, hold = worst_margin − shops) ties cma-1 (+34.9 on 107 shared maps, t ≈ 0.9). So gp-7/cma-7 gains must come from new moves, chiefly travel-time routes and bypasses, then motorways, and from less noise. Swapping the fitness from trips to survival time or log trips does not reduce noise (paired t-statistics are identical).
FINDINGS:
1. [high] Trips depend almost entirely on survival time; throughput while alive is the same for every rule.

3. [medium] The wall is a car-supply squeeze from the demand ramp and the shop wave, so shorter round trips are the main lever.

   evidence: Different rules on the same map correlate at about r = 0.09 (mean of the 28 rule pairs on 7000–7099, h2.py). 11. [low] Phase-dependent ('dynamic') policies are possible but have little direct evidence of value; any payoff must come at the wall.
   evidence: GLOBAL_TERMS include time (scale 4096), shops, houses, next_offer_s and worst_margin (gp.py:61-66, 87). Throughput while alive is identical across rules and early deaths are worth less than 10 trips, so phase-specific behaviour can only help through the network it leaves for the 1400–1600 s squeeze.

Give CMA a few interaction features (e.g. is_separated×phase, path_cost_s×phase) rather than doubling its 81 weights; its rank reliability on f34 is already low. 12. [medium] The planner lacks per-shop stress information and acts late.

### Investigation: literature
SUMMARY: I reviewed the literature on 14 method families and checked them against the gp-6 and cma-6 caches, using small read-only Python scripts in the scratchpad. Three facts from our data set the priorities, and none of them is about representation. (1) Every game ends with a shop failing, between about 1200 and 1900 simulated seconds. No game comes near the 3600 s cap. Trips track survival time almost exactly (r=0.95; trips/s vs seconds r=0.99), so the objective is one noisy time-to-failure. (2) Unrelated rules agree only weakly on the same map (median r=0.20 over 522 gp-6 rule pairs). Close CMA relatives agree more (r=0.56). (3) Selection bias is large. A cma-6 generation's best-of-16 on 8 maps scores on average 99.5 trips less on validation. Newly validated gp-6 challengers lost 39 to 174 trips. Validated cma-6 best samples averaged 986, below the starting mean's 1027.7. The current stop rule is underpowered. With a paired SD of about 450 at alpha 0.005 and power 0.8, a +50-trip gain needs about 950 maps, and the 50 validation maps only resolve gains of about 200 trips. The literature points the same way. Every successful noisy-optimisation recipe either reduces variance (common random numbers via separate random streams, mirrored sampling) or spends evaluations adaptively (racing, successive halving, OCBA, knowledge gradient). Those recipes also average the search distribution or the archive rather than trusting the best single sample (the pycma docs, UH-CMA-ES, LRA-CMA-ES, Deep-Grid/archive-sampling QD). The GP hyper-heuristic job-shop methods transfer well: phenotypic-characterisation KNN surrogates, feature selection, multi-tree, instance rotation. But their simulations are far less noisy per evaluation than ours, so their claimed speed-ups (about 1/3 training time, under 10% training time) will be smaller here. LLM-guided heuristic evolution (FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, AlphaEvolve) is a credible bold option, because our autopsy output can act as the 'reflection' signal. It does nothing about noise, though, and needs an API plus the user's consent. My recommended order is: variance reduction at the simulator, then a fixed statistical protocol (elitist racing, mean recommendation, a larger fresh validation pool), then a hybrid of GP-built features and a CMA-tuned linear head, plus a surrogate pre-screen, with QD, LLM, curriculum and lexicase as later or optional pieces. Scripts are in <old-machine scratch folder> (noise.py, pair2.py, curse.py, cnt.py, wall.py).
FINDINGS:
1. [high] Our objective is a single time-to-failure. Every game ends with a shop failing at about 1200-1900 s simulated, far below the 3600 s cap, and trips are almost exactly survival time times a near-constant rate.
   evidence: From scratchpad noise.py, pair2.py and wall.py on gp-6/cache.jsonl (4276 f34 games) and cma-6/cache.jsonl (1796). truncated=0 in both. corr(trips, seconds)=0.951 (gp-6) and 0.948 (cma-6). corr(trips/s, seconds)=0.99. Longest game 1986 s. The 3 best rules with 58 games each: seconds q10/q50/q90 = 1254/1428/1633, 1197/1438/1604, 1191/1442/1614. failed_shop is set in 58/58 games. 16% of gp-6 games end before 900 s, against 4% in cma-6.
   implication: A result 'far above baselines' means pushing the failure wall past about 1600 s. Short-cap low-fidelity runs can only screen out junk (gp-6 q10 is 303 s). They cannot rank strong rules, because all strong rules survive the first ~1200 s. Rate-style proxies carry no information beyond survival (r=0.99). A multi-fidelity design would need a different cheap proxy, for example a harder or faster-growth scenario or mid-game snapshots, and its rank correlation with the real game would have to be measured first.
2. [high] How well two rules agree on the same map depends on how related they are. Unrelated rules agree weakly (r≈0.2), close CMA relatives moderately (r≈0.55), and the map itself explains 29-49% of variance. So pairing helps local search much more than global search.
   evidence: pair2.py and noise.py. gp-6: 522 rule pairs sharing ≥12 seeds, median r=0.20 for trips, seconds and rate alike. cma-6: 45 pairs (validated near-neighbours), median r=0.56. Between-seed variance share (seeds with ≥10 rules): gp-6 0.29, cma-6 0.49. Cause: one shared SimulationRandom per world (simulation/Assets/Simulation/TrafficWorld.cs:76; ReferenceRandom.cs:31-37), and PLAN.md:201 says actions consume the shared generator.
   implication: Within a generation, every candidate should play the same maps, as cma-6 and gp-6 round 1 already do. Comparisons through a fixed unrelated reference, as in the gp-6 hall of fame, add variance: Var(A-B)=2σ²(1-r)=1.6σ² at r=0.2, more than σ². Mirrored or antithetic pairs on the same maps should work well for CMA, since its neighbours have r≈0.55.
3. [high] Selection bias dominates both runs. The best training sample is mostly a lucky one, so recommending the best sample loses to recommending the distribution mean or incumbent.
   evidence: curse.py on cma-6/generations.jsonl: train best (8 maps) minus validation of that same sample = 107, 192, 116, 89, 159, -102, 194, 101, 109, 29, mean 99.5 trips. Validated best samples averaged about 986, against 1027.7 for x0 (the cma-1 weights, i.e. the CMA mean at gen 0). On gp-6 generations.jsonl, newly validated challengers' racing mean minus validation: +77, +174, +96, +39, +112, -20. pycma NoiseHandler docs: 'take mean value, the best solution is totally off' (https://cma-es.github.io/apidocs-pycma/cma.optimization_tools.NoiseHandler.html). Expected max of 16 draws ≈ 1.77σ of an 8-map mean (≈70-100 trips), which matches the gap.
   implication: cma-7 should validate and return the CMA distribution mean (es.mean, pycma xfavorite), not each generation's best sample. gp-7 should rank elites on accumulated evaluations (dozens to hundreds of maps per elite), not on a 16-map snapshot.
4. [high] The current stop rule cannot detect realistic gains: 50 validation maps at alpha 0.005 resolve only about 200-trip gains between unrelated rules.
   evidence: cnt.py, sample size n=((2.576+0.842)·SD/δ)², one-sided alpha 0.005, power 0.8. Paired SD 450: δ=50 needs 947 maps, δ=100 needs 237. SD 231 (close mutant): δ=50 needs 250 maps. SD 156 (what CRN with r≈0.8 would give): δ=50 needs 114 maps. Budget: about 450 games/hour on 12 workers, so about 10.8k games/day.
   implication: Without variance reduction, a 5% gain (+50 trips) costs one laptop-hour or more per comparison, and ≥3 runs per method (the PLAN.md requirement) is out of reach. Options: (a) reduce paired SD (CRN mode), (b) use a larger fresh validation pool (≥200 maps never used) with sequential testing that adds maps only for close calls, (c) aim for gains ≥100 trips. Fix the protocol before changing the method.
5. [medium] Common random numbers through separate random streams is the standard remedy for exactly this pairing failure, and probably the highest-leverage change available.
   evidence: In simulation optimisation, CRN only works when draws are synchronised: each source of randomness gets its own stream, so the same draw serves the same purpose in every policy (L'Ecuyer streams; Glasserman & Yao 1992, https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4261/glasserman_yao_guidelines.pdf; Wikipedia summary https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variance_reduction). CRN can be combined with ranking and selection (Kim & Nelson 2001; Chen 2012). Our simulator uses one stream for everything (TrafficWorld.cs:76). Close relatives already reach r≈0.55 even with the single stream.
   implication: Propose a simulator flag, used for training and selection only, that gives house/shop spawn, lot type, offer, demand phase and traffic each their own keyed stream. Ideally each draw would be indexed by event (for example the k-th spawn attempt), so a road blocking one cell does not shift later draws. Each stream's distribution is unchanged, so the expected objective stays the same; only the coupling between runs changes. Keep test seeds 7000-7099 on fidelity mode. Pilot before committing: 2 unrelated rules × 50 seeds in both modes ≈ 200 games ≈ 30 min. If r rises from 0.2 to ≥0.6, the maps needed per comparison fall 2-3x. This needs coordination with Mohammad and the fidelity effort. The remaining unknown is how much chaos stays in traffic and failure timing.
6. [medium] CMA-ES under noise: UH-CMA-ES, PSA-CMA-ES, LRA-CMA-ES and mirrored sampling are all in or near pycma. LRA plus mean recommendation suits cma-7 best.
   evidence: UH-CMA-ES (Hansen, Niederberger, Guzzella, Koumoutsakos 2009, IEEE TEVC) detects noise from rank changes on re-evaluated solutions and then raises evaluations or sigma; pycma has it as cma.NoiseHandler (https://cma-es.github.io/apidocs-pycma/cma.optimization_tools.NoiseHandler.html). PSA-CMA-ES (Nishida & Akimoto, GECCO 2018, https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/gecco/NishidaA18.html) grows λ when the update looks inaccurate. LRA-CMA-ES (Nomura, Akimoto, Ono, GECCO 2023 / ACM TELO 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03473, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15876) adapts the learning rate to hold the update's signal-to-noise ratio constant; under noise it 'continued to improve' where fixed rates stalled, and it handles noise and multimodality with the default λ. Mirrored sampling: Brockhoff et al. PPSN 2010 (https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/inria-00530202); antithetic pairs in OpenAI-ES (https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03864). My estimate for cma-6: true spread among sampled candidates about 20 trips (SD of validated best samples ≈35, minus ≈29 validation noise after map pairing), against about 72 trips residual noise on an 8-map mean. That gives a per-candidate SNR of about 0.28, so the ranks CMA receives are mostly noise.
   implication: cma-7 design: λ=16 as 8 mirrored pairs (m±σz) on the same 8-16 maps per generation, LRA-CMA-ES (Nomura's 'cmaes' package has lr_adapt=True; pycma would need a smaller c_mean) or PSA, rank-based tell, and validation of the mean every k generations. Possibly reduce the 81 dimensions by screening features out (fewer weights, better SNR per update). For a fair comparison with GP, add linear remove and place heads so CMA can also remove roads and place lights and roundabouts.
7. [high] Racing and bandit allocation (irace elitist racing, successive halving, Hyperband, OCBA, knowledge gradient) is a direct fit. The elitist variant, where elites keep and grow their evaluations across generations, matters most.
   evidence: irace (López-Ibáñez et al. 2016, Oper. Res. Perspectives 3:43-58, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.orp.2016.09.002) adds elitist racing: elites keep their instance results, and new configurations must race them on the same instances. Hyperband (Li et al., JMLR 18(185) 2018, https://jmlr.org/papers/v18/16-558.html) runs successive-halving brackets. OCBA for picking an elite subset inside an EA: LaPorte & Branke WSC 2012 (https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc12papers/includes/files/con371.pdf). Knowledge gradient: Frazier, Powell & Dayanik, SIAM J. Control Optim. 2008 (https://optimallearning.princeton.edu/Papers/Frazier%20Powell%20-%20Knowledge%20gradient%20policy.pdf). Adaptive sampling in GAs: Cantú-Paz GECCO 2004 (https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-24854-5_95) removes the need to fix a sample size, but can cost a lot. Our cache is deterministic per (rule, seed), so replaying old maps is free.
   implication: gp-7: a shared map pool that rotates slowly (for example 16 new maps per generation added to a window of about 64-128). Every elite and hall-of-fame member keeps every game it has played on pool maps. Offspring then pass successive-halving rungs: 64 offspring × 4 maps, top 16 get +8, top 4 get +24, and only a challenger whose paired lower confidence bound against the incumbent on the shared maps exceeds 0 goes to validation. This is about 64·4+16·8+4·24 ≈ 480 games/generation, close to gp-6's ~390, with far more breadth and a much sharper incumbent estimate.
8. [medium] Surrogate-assisted GP with phenotypic characterisation (PC) is the best-supported GP hyper-heuristic accelerator. Here it also has a second use: averaging the noisy fitness of behaviourally similar rules.
   evidence: Hildebrandt & Branke, 'On Using Surrogates with GP', Evol. Comput. 23(3) 2015 (https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Hildebrandt_2014_EC.html): the PC is the rule's decisions on a fixed set of decision situations, with a nearest-neighbour surrogate on top; faster convergence and better quality for dispatching rules. Zhang, Mei, Nguyen, Zhang, Tan, 'Surrogate-assisted evolutionary multitask GP', IEEE TEVC 25(4) 2021 (https://openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/journal_contribution/Surrogate-Assisted_Evolutionary_Multitask_Genetic_Programming_for_Dynamic_Flexible_Job_Shop_Scheduling/19704499). Instance-rotation surrogate, TEVC 2023 (https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Fangfang_Zhang_ieeeTEC4.html): uses samples from across generations even when training instances rotate. Zhu et al. GECCO 2023 (https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Zhu_2023_GECCO.html): comparable quality with about 1/3 of the training time. Mei et al. 2017 TETCI feature selection with niching plus surrogate: under 10% of training time (https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Mei_2017_ieeeETCI.html). Deep-Grid MAP-Elites estimates a solution's fitness from an archive of similar solutions in noisy domains (https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:181C4920). Training data we already have: 285 distinct gp-6 rules (4287 games) and 163 cma-6 rules (1799 games) (cnt.py).
   implication: Record a decision bank once: about 300-500 candidate-choice situations from cma-1 games on about 20 non-validation maps (the feature dicts and candidate lists). PC = the rank each rule gives the reference rule's choice in each situation, plus hold, upgrade, remove and place decisions. Computing a PC needs no games. Uses: (1) a KNN surrogate pre-screens 5-10x more offspring, and only the top predicted ones play; (2) a KNN-smoothed fitness pools games of PC-neighbours to reduce noise; (3) PC distance replaces behaviour-hash de-dup and drives niching. Caveat: the cited speed-ups come from job-shop simulations with low noise per evaluation, so expect less here.
9. [medium] Combining GP-built features with a linear model (MRGP, linear scaling, FFX, GP+CMA for constants) fits our evidence: the linear CMA rule is the strongest, and GP's best results are either small (29 nodes) or mutants of the linear seed.
   evidence: MRGP (Arnaldo, Krawiec, O'Reilly, GECCO 2014, https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Arnaldo_2014_GECCO.html) fits a linear model over a program's subexpressions and beats both GP and multiple regression at equal budget. Keijzer's linear scaling (https://www.cs.york.ac.uk/rts/docs/GECCO_2005/Workshop%20and%20tutorials/gecco05/papers/28.pdf). FFX (McConaghy GPTP 2011, https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/McConaghy_2011_GPTP.html). Bruneton et al. evolve structure with GP and fit scalars with CMA-ES, which they say gets costly above about 60 elements (https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.03959). Multi-tree GP beats cooperative coevolution with smaller rules (Zhang, Mei, Zhang AJCAI 2018, https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Zhang_2018_AJCAImt.html). Our data: f34 test cma-1 981 vs gp-4 841; gp-6 never beat the cma-1 seed (1027.7).
   implication: There are no supervised targets here, so MRGP's least squares does not carry over. Use an alternating scheme instead. The policy is the cma-1 linear head plus K≤4 small GP feature trees (depth ≤4) appended as extra terms with their own weights. Setting the new weights to 0 recovers the baseline exactly, so the search space contains it. GP varies only the feature trees. Weights for new features are set by a short antithetic ±w test on shared maps, and every few rounds a CMA-ES re-tunes the whole head. This is a cleaner GP-vs-CMA story (does GP-built structure add value on top of the best linear model?) and avoids GP relearning the 321-node linear rule.
10. [medium] Instance rotation and how many maps to use per generation is studied in the GP hyper-heuristic literature. Mini-batches combined with rotation generalise better than single-instance rotation.
   evidence: Xu, Zhang, Mei & Zhang, CEC 2022, multi-case fitness (https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Xu_2022_CEC2.html): one instance per generation discards individuals that happened to draw a bad instance. Yang et al., Inf. Sci. 2024, workflow scheduling (https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Yang_2024_ins.html): random mini-batches beat rotation at equal cost, and combining both is better still. Generalisation across instance types: Zhu, Zhang, Mei & Zhang 2026 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15717).
   implication: This supports the sliding shared pool (a mini-batch plus slow rotation) over fully fresh maps each generation. Fresh maps make old elite scores incomparable across generations; a sliding pool keeps them comparable.
11. [medium] Quality diversity (MAP-Elites) for rule discovery has job-shop and project-scheduling evidence, but under noise the archive fills with lucky elites unless it is re-sampled.
   evidence: QDGP for dynamic flexible job shop (Xu, Neumann, Neumann, Ong, GECCO 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02235): better best-found heuristic than standard GP. MAP-Elites hyper-heuristic for RCPSP (Chand et al. 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11162). Uncertain QD (Flageat & Cully, IEEE TEVC 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00463): Archive-sampling, Parallel-Adaptive-sampling and Deep-Grid are needed because noisy fitness and noisy descriptors corrupt the archive. DSevolve (2026 preprint, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27628) evolves a QD portfolio of rules with LLMs and switches between them online.
   implication: Medium-low priority. If used: a small grid (for example 4×4 over lights+roundabouts placed and roads removed, or tiles_built × trips_per_tile), with elites re-sampled each generation as in Archive-sampling, feeding parents to the main racing loop. It mainly protects diversity, which de-dup plus immigrants partly does already.
12. [medium] Lexicase selection (epsilon, down-sampled, informed) fits poorly when the cases are noisy single-map games.
   evidence: Down-sampled lexicase (Hernandez, Lalejini, Dolson, Ofria, GECCO'19 companion; Boldi et al. informed down-sampling, https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01488) and epsilon-lexicase (La Cava et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13266) were shown on program synthesis and regression, where cases are deterministic or low-noise. Moore & Stanton's lexicase in evolutionary robotics uses deterministic environment variants and breaks down when environment sampling is extreme (https://eprints.keele.ac.uk/6710/; https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/45521). In our data, unrelated rules agree only at r=0.2 on the same map, so the 'best on this case' set is mostly noise.
   implication: Do not use lexicase for survival selection. At most, epsilon-lexicase for parent selection only (not survival) over normalised per-map survival, as a diversity device, if CRN mode raises the same-map agreement substantially.
13. [medium] 'Dynamic GP' variants: the valuable one is dynamic sample sizing, which grows evaluations as candidates converge. Time- or state-switched policies and adaptive operator rates offer less here.
   evidence: Noisy-EA surveys: Jin & Branke 2005 IEEE TEVC (https://www.cs.le.ac.uk/people/sy11/ECiDUE/JinBranke-TEVC05.pdf); Rakshit, Konar & Das 2017 (https://next.openmole.org/paper/rakshit2016.pdf). Both list resampling, fitness estimation and dynamic population sizing as the main tools, and say larger populations or resampling reduce noise effects. State-based rule selection: Đurasević & Jakobović select GP rules by instance features; Pickardt et al. 2012 assign rules to work centres (https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Pickardt_2012_IJPE.html). Ensembles of dispatching rules (Park et al. EuroGP 2015/2016, https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Park_2015_EuroGP.html) were more robust in static job shop, but a single-population version showed no gain (https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Park_2015_GECCOcomp.html). Our trees already have a 'time' terminal and if_gt, so phase-switching is already expressible.
   implication: Make the sample size dynamic, via racing rungs and accumulating elites. Explicit early/mid/late trees should wait until autopsy shows failure modes specific to one phase. Every game ends at about 1200-1900 s, so failures may cluster in the late phase, and a specialised 'late-game' head could be where a large gain lies. Check with autopsy.py before building it.
14. [low] LLM-guided heuristic evolution (FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, AlphaEvolve, LLM dispatching-rule papers) is a credible bold option. Our autopsy output is a natural 'reflection' signal, but evaluation noise still limits it.
   evidence: FunSearch (Romera-Paredes et al., Nature 2024, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6): LLM plus evaluator with island evolution; beat classic online bin-packing heuristics. EoH (Liu et al., ICML 2024, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/liu24bs.html): evolves the idea in natural language together with the code, with far fewer LLM queries than FunSearch. ReEvo (Ye et al., NeurIPS 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01145): reflections act as 'verbal gradients' and are more sample-efficient. AlphaEvolve (2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13131): needs programmatically verifiable problems. Dynamic job shop: SeEvo (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22657) and DSevolve (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27628) report beating GP and DRL, but both are preprints. An AAAI paper notes EoH's objective is unstable on black-box problems (https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34898/37053).
   implication: As a side arm, not the main learner. The LLM proposes priority/hold/upgrade formulas over our documented terminals, or small Python feature functions, given the incumbent, per-map scores and autopsy summaries (failed_shop, stuck cars). Each proposal goes through the same racing rungs. The budget is noise-bound: at about 50 games per reliable estimate that is about 9 candidates/hour, about 200/day. It needs an API key, the user's consent (code and feature names leave the machine), and a separate report so the GP-vs-CMA comparison stays clean.
15. [medium] Curriculum and unsupervised environment design (PLR, ACCEL, SFL learnability) is mature in RL but answers a different bottleneck. It changes the training distribution and could bias selection away from the uniform LA objective.
   evidence: ACCEL (Parker-Holder et al. 2022, https://accelagent.github.io) mutates curated high-regret levels. PLR replays levels with high estimated regret. SFL (Rutherford et al., NeurIPS 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15099) shows regret approximations mostly track success rate. It trains on levels solved 'sometimes but not always' and beats DR, PLR and ACCEL on four environments. Informed down-sampling (Boldi et al.) is the GP analogue of choosing informative cases.
   implication: Keep it for later, as the advisor suggested. A cheap GP-side version: oversample 'discriminative' training maps (high between-rule variance or early failures) for exploration, but always do final selection and validation on uniform maps. With r≈0.2, a map's discriminativeness is itself noisy, so this probably only pays off once CRN mode exists.

OPEN QUESTIONS:
- How much does a separate-stream CRN mode actually raise same-map agreement between unrelated rules? Traffic dynamics and failure timing may stay chaotic even with synchronised spawns. A pilot of about 200 games would answer it.

- Can the headless host save and restore mid-game state? Forking from snapshots around 900-1200 s would give a cheap, CRN-friendly high-fidelity proxy for the late-game failure phase.

- Is LLM API use acceptable (privacy, cost, the user's consent), and would an LLM arm count as part of the project's method comparison or only as a side experiment?
